Squat Overflow privacy policy

Last updated: 11 November 2026

Squat Overflow is a browser extension that reminds you to take short movement breaks and uses your webcam to count and score the exercises in each break. This page explains what data it handles, where that data goes, and how to remove it.

Camera frames

  • During a break, and only after you allow camera access, the extension reads frames from your webcam and finds your body landmarks on your own computer, with a pose model that ships inside the extension (Google's MediaPipe). It counts reps and scores form there too.
  • Camera frames are processed on your device and never sent anywhere: not to our server, not to Google, not to anyone. They are never written to disk, logged or stored.
  • Blurred mode and the avatar characters change the preview on your own screen.

Data stored on your device

Your settings, schedule, XP, level, streak, badges, challenges and break history are kept in the extension's local storage on your computer. They are not sent anywhere unless you sign in, as described below. Uninstalling the extension deletes them.

Sign in with Google (optional)

  • Signing in is optional. You need it only for Competitive mode and for syncing your progress between devices. The extension asks Google for your basic profile (name, email address and profile picture).
  • That sign-in is checked by our server, which keeps an account record in its database with your Google account ID, email address, name and picture, so your leaderboard entry is tied to your verified work email.
  • The sign-in session is stored in the extension's local storage. Signing out in Settings removes it from your device, but the account record stays until you ask us to delete it.

Sync between your devices (signed in only)

  • While you are signed in, the extension and the Squat Overflow apps send our server each finished break (its time, day, reps, moves, form score, XP, and whether it was completed), your shared settings (break interval, areas, active hours, workdays and similar preferences), and your badges and challenges. The server keeps them for your account, so your other signed-in devices show the same progress.
  • Signed out, nothing is synced: everything stays on the device.
  • Camera data is never part of it.

Leaderboard (Competitive mode only)

  • In Competitive mode, the extension sends these to our server, which stores them in its leaderboard database: the display name you choose, your XP, level, streak, weekly XP and chosen avatar.
  • The server adds your company domain (for example example.com), taken from your signed-in work email. Personal addresses such as gmail.com can't join a leaderboard.
  • The leaderboard never shows form scores, break history or camera data.
  • Only signed-in people from the same company domain can see the leaderboard. They see your name, level, XP, streak and avatar.
  • Switching back to Personal mode, or signing out, deletes your leaderboard entry. Do this before you uninstall, or contact us to have the entry removed.

Google Calendar (optional)

  • If you connect Google Calendar, at sign-in or later, the extension asks Google for read-only free/busy times so that it doesn't start a break during a meeting.
  • It cannot see event titles, attendees or details.
  • The access token and the busy times stay in the extension's local storage and are sent only to Google. Disconnecting revokes the token and deletes them.

Exercise demos

  • During a break and on the Practice page you can open a demo of the exercise. It starts as a 3D figure drawn on your device, which needs nothing from the internet.
  • Exercises that have a demo video then play it instead: a short, silent clip downloaded from our server when you open the demo, not before. The request carries no account details and nothing about your breaks or your camera. Like any download, it shows our server your IP address and which exercise's video was asked for; we don't keep a record of it. A video cut from someone else's footage shows a credit under it; tapping the credit opens the original video on its own site (YouTube) in your browser, where that site's privacy policy applies.
  • Offline, or in a copy of the extension with no server set, the figure stays.

What we don't do

  • No advertising, analytics or tracking.
  • No selling or transferring of your data to third parties, other than the service providers named above, which are used only to run these features.
  • No reading of the web pages you visit. The extension looks at the address of tabs that are playing sound, only to spot a meeting site (Google Meet, Zoom or Teams) so that it doesn't interrupt a call. It also checks tab addresses to find its own break window. Addresses are checked on your device and never sent anywhere.

Changes and contact
